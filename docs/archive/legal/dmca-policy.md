## 2. Copyright Infringement Notification (Takedown Notice)

If you believe that your copyrighted work has been copied and is accessible on the Flamoral Service in a way that constitutes copyright infringement, you may submit a DMCA takedown notice.

### 2.1 Requirements for a Valid DMCA Notice

Under the DMCA (17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3)), your notice must include:

1. **Physical or electronic signature** of the copyright owner or person authorized to act on their behalf

2. **Identification of the copyrighted work** claimed to have been infringed
   - If multiple works, a representative list of such works

3. **Identification of the infringing material** and information sufficient to locate it
   - Specific URLs or locations on our Service
   - Screenshots or descriptions if helpful
   - User profile links if applicable

4. **Your contact information:**
   - Name
   - Address
   - Phone number
   - Email address

5. **Statement of good faith belief:**
   - A statement that you have a good faith belief that use of the material is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law

6. **Statement of accuracy and authority:**
   - A statement under penalty of perjury that:
     - The information in your notice is accurate
     - You are the copyright owner or authorized to act on the owner's behalf

7. **Your physical or electronic signature**

### 2.2 Sample DMCA Notice Format

```
To: Flamoral DMCA Agent
Email: dmca@flamoral.com

I, [Your Name], hereby certify that:

1. I am the copyright owner (or authorized to act on behalf of the owner) of the
   following copyrighted work(s):

   [Description of copyrighted work]
   [Copyright registration number, if available]

2. The following material on the Flamoral Service infringes the copyrighted work:

   [Specific URL(s) or location(s) of infringing material]
   [Description of how the material infringes]

3. My contact information:

   Name: [Your Full Name]
   Address: [Your Address]
   Phone: [Your Phone Number]
   Email: [Your Email Address]

4. I have a good faith belief that use of the copyrighted material described above
   is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.

5. I declare, under penalty of perjury, that the information in this notification
   is accurate and that I am the copyright owner or authorized to act on behalf
   of the owner.

Signature: [Your Physical or Electronic Signature]
Date: [Date]
```

Upon receiving a valid DMCA notice, we will:

## 3. Counter-Notification (DMCA Counter-Notice)

If you believe that material you posted was removed in error or misidentification, you may submit a DMCA counter-notice.

### 3.1 Requirements for a Valid Counter-Notice

Under the DMCA (17 U.S.C. § 512(g)), your counter-notice must include:

1. **Your physical or electronic signature**

2. **Identification of the material** that was removed or disabled
   - URL or location before removal
   - Description of the material

3. **Statement under penalty of perjury:**
   - A statement that you have a good faith belief that the material was removed or disabled as a result of mistake or misidentification

4. **Your contact information:**
   - Name
   - Address
   - Phone number
   - Email address

5. **Consent to jurisdiction:**
   - A statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the Federal District Court for the judicial district in which your address is located (or the District of Delaware if you are outside the United States)
   - A statement that you will accept service of process from the person who provided the original DMCA notice or their agent

6. **Your physical or electronic signature**

### 3.2 Sample Counter-Notice Format

```
To: Flamoral DMCA Agent
Email: dmca@flamoral.com

Re: Counter-Notification Under DMCA

I, [Your Name], hereby submit this counter-notification:

1. Identification of material removed:

   [URL or description of material that was removed]
   [Date of removal]

2. I declare, under penalty of perjury, that I have a good faith belief that
   the material was removed or disabled as a result of mistake or
   misidentification.

3. My contact information:

   Name: [Your Full Name]
   Address: [Your Address]
   Phone: [Your Phone Number]
   Email: [Your Email Address]

4. I consent to the jurisdiction of the Federal District Court for the judicial
   district in which my address is located [or District of Delaware if outside
   the United States], and I will accept service of process from the person who
   provided the DMCA notification or their agent.

Signature: [Your Physical or Electronic Signature]
Date: [Date]
```

### 3.3 How to Submit a Counter-Notice

Submit to the same contact information as DMCA notices:
- Email: dmca@flamoral.com
- Subject: "DMCA Counter-Notice"

### 3.4 What Happens After We Receive a Counter-Notice

Upon receiving a valid counter-notice:

1. **We forward your counter-notice** to the original complainant
2. **We wait 10-14 business days**
3. **If the complainant does not file a lawsuit** within that time:
   - We restore the removed material
   - We reinstate your access
4. **If the complainant files a lawsuit:**
   - The material remains removed
   - The dispute is resolved in court

**Important:** By submitting a counter-notice, you are indicating that you are willing to defend your position in federal court if necessary.

## 4. Repeat Infringer Policy

### 4.1 Three-Strike Policy

Flamoral has adopted a policy to terminate, in appropriate circumstances, users who are repeat infringers.

**First Violation:**
- Content removed
- Warning issued
- Account flagged

**Second Violation:**
- Content removed
- Temporary account suspension (7-30 days)
- Final warning

**Third Violation:**
- Permanent account termination
- All content removed
- Ban from creating new accounts

### 4.2 Exceptions

We may terminate an account after fewer violations if:
- The infringement is particularly egregious
- The user is engaged in commercial piracy
- The user refuses to cooperate with takedown requests
- Legal action has been initiated

### 4.3 Appeals

Users may appeal termination by:
- Emailing appeals@flamoral.com
- Providing evidence that:
  - The material was not infringing
  - The takedown notice was invalid
  - They have obtained proper authorization
- Agreeing to additional monitoring if reinstated

## 5. Misrepresentation

### 5.1 False DMCA Claims

Under the DMCA, **you may be liable for damages** (including costs and attorneys' fees) if you knowingly materially misrepresent that:
- Material is infringing, or
- Material was removed or disabled by mistake or misidentification

### 5.2 Consequences of False Claims

If you submit a false DMCA notice:
- You may be liable for damages to the user
- You may be liable for damages to Flamoral
- You may be subject to criminal penalties under 17 U.S.C. § 512(f)
- Your future DMCA notices may receive less priority

### 5.3 Good Faith Requirement

Before submitting a DMCA notice:
- Ensure you own the copyright or are authorized to act
- Verify that the material is actually infringing
- Consider whether the use might be fair use
- Ensure your notice is complete and accurate

## 6. What is Not Copyright Infringement

Not all uses of copyrighted material constitute infringement. Consider the following:

### 6.1 Fair Use

Under U.S. law, some uses of copyrighted material are considered "fair use" and are not infringing. Fair use factors include:
- Purpose and character of the use (commercial vs. educational)
- Nature of the copyrighted work
- Amount and substantiality of the portion used
- Effect on the market for the original work

**Examples that may be fair use:**
- Commentary or criticism
- Parody
- News reporting
- Educational use
- Transformative use

### 6.2 Public Domain

Material in the public domain is not protected by copyright:
- Works with expired copyrights
- Works by the U.S. government
- Works explicitly placed in the public domain by the creator

### 6.3 Licensed Content

Material used with permission is not infringing:
- Creative Commons licensed content (if used according to the license)
- Content you have a license to use
- Content you created yourself

### 6.4 Think Before You File

Before submitting a DMCA notice, ask yourself:
- Do I own the copyright to this work?
- Is the use actually infringing or could it be fair use?
- Has the user licensed or been given permission to use the material?
- Is the material in the public domain?

## 7. Specific Situations

### 7.1 Profile Photos

If someone uses your photo without permission:
- File a DMCA notice if you own the copyright (you took the photo or own the rights)
- If the photo was taken by someone else, they own the copyright
- You may also report under our Terms of Service for impersonation

### 7.2 Artwork and Graphics

If someone uses your original artwork:
- File a DMCA notice
- Include links to your original work
- Provide proof of copyright ownership (registration, portfolio, etc.)

### 7.3 Written Content

If someone copies your written content (bio, profile text, etc.):
- Copyright protects original written works
- File a DMCA notice with specific copied text
- Note: Short phrases and common expressions may not be copyrightable

### 7.4 Music and Videos

If someone uses your music or video:
- File a DMCA notice if you own the rights
- Provide links to original content
- Note: Cover songs or remixes may be fair use or require separate analysis

### 7.5 Trademarks

**Note:** DMCA only covers copyright, not trademarks. For trademark issues:
- Email: legal@flamoral.com
- Subject: "Trademark Infringement"
- Provide trademark registration information
- Describe how the mark is being used

## 15. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: How long does it take to process a DMCA notice?

**A:** Most notices are processed within 2-3 business days. Urgent cases may be handled within 24 hours. Complex cases may take up to 7 business days.

### Q2: What if I can't find the specific URL of the infringing content?

**A:** Provide as much detail as possible: user profile link, description of the content, approximate date posted, any identifying information. Screenshots are helpful.

### Q3: Can I file a DMCA notice for content that's similar to mine but not exactly the same?

**A:** DMCA applies to copies of your work. Similar content may not be infringing. If the content is substantially similar and derivative, consult an attorney about whether it infringes.

### Q4: What if someone is using my photo but I didn't take it?

**A:** The photographer owns the copyright. You would need the photographer to file the DMCA notice, or you need proof that you own the rights to the photo.

### Q5: Can I withdraw a DMCA notice?

**A:** Yes. Email dmca@flamoral.com stating that you wish to withdraw your notice. Include the original notice details. We will restore the content if it was removed.

### Q6: What happens if I file a counter-notice and the complainant sues me?

**A:** The dispute moves to federal court. You should consult an attorney immediately. Flamoral will comply with any court orders but is not a party to the dispute.

### Q7: How do I know if my use is fair use?

**A:** Fair use is complex and fact-specific. When in doubt, consult an attorney. Generally, transformative uses, commentary, criticism, and educational uses are more likely to be fair use.

### Q8: Will you tell the user who filed the DMCA notice?

**A:** Yes. We provide a copy of the DMCA notice to the user whose content was removed. Your contact information (as included in the notice) will be shared.

### Q9: Can I file a DMCA notice anonymously?

**A:** No. DMCA notices require your contact information and signature. This information is shared with the alleged infringer.

### Q10: What if the same user keeps re-uploading infringing content?

**A:** File a new DMCA notice for each instance. Repeat infringers may have their accounts terminated under our Repeat Infringer Policy.
