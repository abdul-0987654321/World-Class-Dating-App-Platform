## 1. Online Safety

### 1.1 Getting Started Safely

**Create a Strong Password**
- Use at least 12 characters
- Combine uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols
- Don't reuse passwords from other accounts
- Consider using a password manager
- Enable two-factor authentication if available

**Set Up Your Profile Thoughtfully**
- Use recent, accurate photos of yourself
- Be honest about who you are
- Don't include identifying information (last name, workplace, address)
- Avoid photos that show your location (house number, street signs)
- Review privacy settings to control who can see your profile

**Take Your Time**
- Don't rush into sharing personal details
- Get to know someone through the app first
- Ask questions and have meaningful conversations
- Watch for inconsistencies in their story
- Trust your gut feelings

### 1.2 Communication Best Practices

**Keep Early Conversations on Flamoral**
- Use the in-app messaging until you're comfortable
- Don't feel pressured to share your phone number immediately
- The platform has safety features to protect you
- We can help if there are issues

**Video Chat First**
- Use video calls before meeting in person
- Verify they look like their photos
- Confirm they are who they claim to be
- Assess comfort level through conversation
- Note: Be wary if they always have excuses not to video chat

**Pace Yourself**
- Don't feel rushed to meet someone
- Take time to build trust
- It's okay to stay in the messaging phase as long as you want
- A genuine person will respect your pace

**Watch What You Share**
- Don't share your:
  - Full name (until you're comfortable)
  - Home or work address
  - Phone number (too soon)
  - Email address
  - Financial information
  - Social media profiles (until ready)
  - Daily routines or schedules
  - Travel plans
  - Intimate photos

## 2. Protecting Your Personal Information

### 2.1 What NOT to Share (Initially)

**Never share:**
- Social Security Number
- Bank account or credit card information
- Passwords
- ID documents
- Tax information
- Home address
- Workplace address

**Be cautious sharing:**
- Full legal name
- Phone number
- Email address
- Social media profiles
- Specific workplace details
- Where you regularly spend time
- Your children's information

### 2.2 Protecting Your Identity

**Use a Google Voice or temporary number:**
- When ready to share a phone number, consider using Google Voice
- This protects your real number
- You can block the number easily if needed

**Create a separate email:**
- Use a dedicated email for online dating
- Keep your primary email private
- This helps separate your dating life from other accounts

**Limit social media exposure:**
- Review your social media privacy settings
- Make profiles private
- Be cautious about who you accept as friends/followers
- Don't use the same photos on Flamoral and social media (reverse image search)

**Be aware of photo metadata:**
- Photos can contain location data (GPS coordinates)
- Remove metadata before sharing photos
- Many apps automatically strip this data, but be aware

### 2.3 Protecting Your Location

**Don't share your exact location:**
- Use general location (city, not specific neighborhood)
- Don't check in on social media when meeting someone
- Avoid posting real-time location updates
- Be vague about your exact area

**Be careful with background details:**
- Avoid photos showing street signs
- Don't show recognizable landmarks near your home
- Be mindful of visible addresses or identifying features
- Consider what's visible through windows in photos

## 3. Recognizing Red Flags and Scams

### 3.1 Common Warning Signs

**Profile Red Flags:**
- Photos look like model or professional shots
- Photos seem inconsistent or from different people
- Profile has limited information
- Bio seems generic or too good to be true
- Recently created account with immediate interest in you
- Claims to live nearby but is "temporarily abroad"

**Behavior Red Flags:**
- Immediately professes strong feelings ("love bombing")
- Pushes to move off the platform quickly
- Avoids answering questions about themselves
- Story changes or has inconsistencies
- Refuses to video chat (always has excuses)
- Asks lots of personal questions but shares little
- Pressures you for photos (especially intimate ones)
- Makes you feel uncomfortable or rushed
- Gets angry if you set boundaries
- Love bombs then withdraws (manipulation)

**Financial Red Flags:**
- Asks for money (for ANY reason)
- Has a "financial emergency"
- Wants help with investments or business
- Asks you to receive/send packages
- Requests gift cards or cryptocurrency
- Claims to need money to visit you
- Asks for help paying bills
- Wants your bank information
- Offers too-good-to-be-true investment opportunities

### 3.2 Common Scams

**Romance Scams:**
1. Scammer creates fake profile
2. Builds emotional connection quickly
3. Claims to be traveling or in military
4. Creates a crisis (medical emergency, stuck abroad, etc.)
5. Asks for money
6. May ask for money repeatedly
7. Disappears once you stop sending money

**Red flags:**
- Rapid emotional escalation
- Claims to be deployed overseas
- "Stuck" in another country
- Medical emergency
- Needs money to visit you
- Job requires them to travel constantly

**Catfishing:**
- Using fake photos and identity
- Creating a fictional persona
- Building an emotional relationship under false pretenses
- May or may not involve financial scam

**How to verify:**
- Reverse image search their photos (Google Images, TinEye)
- Video chat to confirm they match photos
- Be suspicious if they always have excuses
- Check if their story is consistent

**Investment Scams:**
- Claims to have inside knowledge
- Cryptocurrency "opportunities"
- Trading platform recommendations
- Promises of high returns
- Asks you to invest or send money

**Warning:** Legitimate romantic interests don't pitch investments.

**Verification Scams:**
- Claims you need to "verify" on another site
- Asks for payment for verification
- Sends link to external "verification" site
- May claim it's for safety

**Truth:** Flamoral verification is free and done in-app. Never pay for verification elsewhere.

**Blackmail/Sextortion:**
- Requests intimate photos
- Threatens to share photos unless you pay
- Claims to have compromising information
- Threatens to contact your family/employer

**If this happens:**
- Don't pay (it won't stop)
- Report to Flamoral immediately
- Report to FBI (ic3.gov)
- Contact local police

### 3.3 How to Verify Someone is Real

**Steps to verify:**

1. **Video chat**
   - See if they match their photos
   - Note if they make excuses to avoid it
   - Have them show something specific (hold up a sign, etc.)

2. **Reverse image search**
   - Save their photos
   - Upload to Google Images or TinEye
   - See if photos appear elsewhere online

3. **Social media check**
   - Search their name on social media
   - Look for profiles that match their story
   - Check if profile seems genuine (friends, posts over time)
   - Be suspicious of new or sparse profiles

4. **Verify their story**
   - Ask detailed questions about their life
   - Note inconsistencies
   - Ask about specific local details if they claim to be from an area

5. **Trust your instincts**
   - If something feels off, it probably is
   - Don't ignore red flags
   - Better safe than sorry

## 4. Meeting in Person Safely

### 4.1 Before You Decide to Meet

**Prerequisites:**
- You've communicated for a reasonable time (at least a few days, ideally longer)
- You've had at least one video chat
- They've been consistent in their communication
- You feel comfortable and safe
- No red flags have appeared
- Your gut feeling is positive

**If you have ANY doubts, wait longer or don't meet.**

### 4.2 Planning the First Meeting

**Choose a Safe Location:**

**Do:**
- Meet in a public place (coffee shop, restaurant, park)
- Choose somewhere with other people around
- Pick a well-lit area
- Select a location you're familiar with
- Meet during daytime or early evening

**Don't:**
- Meet at your home
- Meet at their home
- Meet in isolated locations
- Meet in private spaces
- Meet very late at night

**Plan Your Transportation:**

**Do:**
- Arrange your own transportation
- Drive yourself or use a rideshare
- Know how you'll get home
- Have backup transportation plans
- Keep your car keys accessible

**Don't:**
- Let them pick you up
- Share a ride to the first meeting
- Accept a ride home (initially)
- Leave your car keys with them
- Depend on them for transportation

**Set a Time Limit:**
- Plan a shorter first meeting (coffee, lunch)
- This gives you an easy exit
- You can always extend if it goes well
- Don't commit to an all-day or evening date initially

### 4.3 Tell Someone Where You're Going

**Critical Safety Step:**

**Tell a trusted friend or family member:**
- Who you're meeting (name, profile link)
- Where you're meeting (specific location)
- When you're meeting (date and time)
- When you expect to be back
- Share your live location if possible

**Plan check-ins:**
- Set a specific time to text them
- Agree on a code word for emergency
- Have them call you at a certain time
- Check in when you get home safely

**Use safety apps:**
- Share live location with trusted contacts
- Use apps like Life360, Find My Friends
- Enable emergency SOS on your phone
- Keep your phone charged

### 4.4 During the Date

**Stay Alert:**
- Keep your phone charged and accessible
- Stay sober (limit alcohol)
- Don't leave your drink unattended
- Don't accept drinks you didn't see prepared
- Watch your belongings
- Trust your instincts

**Maintain Boundaries:**
- You can leave anytime for any reason
- You don't owe anyone anything
- Say no if uncomfortable
- Don't feel obligated to continue the date
- Don't feel pressured to go to a second location

**Watch for Warning Signs:**
- They don't look like their photos
- They're much older/younger than stated
- They're intoxicated
- They're aggressive or pushy
- They pressure you to drink
- They try to isolate you from others
- They want to leave the public place
- They make you uncomfortable
- Your gut says something is wrong

**If you feel unsafe:**
1. Trust your instinct
2. Make an excuse to leave (bathroom, emergency call)
3. Don't go to a second location
4. Call your safety contact
5. Go to a safe place (stay where there are people)
6. Don't worry about being rude—your safety comes first

## 5. Date Safety Best Practices

### 5.1 First Date Do's and Don'ts

**Do:**
- Meet in public
- Tell someone where you're going
- Arrange your own transportation
- Keep your phone charged
- Stay relatively sober
- Listen to your intuition
- Set boundaries
- Check in with your safety contact
- Inspect your drink (watch it being made)
- Have cash for transportation
- Know where exits are
- Be ready to leave if needed

**Don't:**
- Give out your address
- Leave your drink unattended
- Get heavily intoxicated
- Accept rides to unknown locations
- Go to their place or invite them to yours
- Share financial information
- Ignore red flags
- Feel obligated to stay
- Accept expensive gifts (creates obligation)
- Leave with someone if you're uncomfortable

### 5.2 Alcohol and Drug Safety

**Be Smart About Alcohol:**
- Limit your intake (stay in control)
- Watch your drinks being made
- Don't accept drinks from strangers
- Don't leave drinks unattended
- Order your own drinks
- Stay alert to your surroundings

**Be Aware of Date Rape Drugs:**
- Rohypnol, GHB, Ketamine, and others
- Can be colorless, tasteless, and odorless
- Can cause memory loss, disorientation, loss of consciousness
- NEVER leave your drink unattended
- If your drink tastes strange, don't drink it
- If you feel suddenly very intoxicated, get help immediately

**If you suspect you've been drugged:**
1. Tell a friend immediately
2. Get to a safe place
3. Call 911 or go to the emergency room
4. Don't go anywhere alone
5. Save any evidence (drink, glass)
6. Get tested (drugs leave the system quickly)

### 5.3 Sexual Consent and Boundaries

**Consent Basics:**
- Consent must be enthusiastic, ongoing, and freely given
- You can change your mind at any time
- Being on a date doesn't obligate you to anything
- You never owe anyone sexual activity
- Intoxication invalidates consent
- Saying "yes" to one thing doesn't mean "yes" to everything

**Setting Boundaries:**
- Clearly communicate your boundaries
- Say no if you're uncomfortable
- Don't be pressured or coerced
- A respectful person will honor your boundaries
- You can leave at any time

**Red Flags:**
- Doesn't respect "no"
- Pressures you for sex
- Gets angry when you set boundaries
- Tries to get you overly intoxicated
- Isolates you from others
- Makes you feel guilty for saying no
- Doesn't take no for an answer

## 6. Reporting and Blocking

### 6.1 When to Report

Report immediately if someone:
- Asks for money
- Sends explicit content without consent
- Harasses or threatens you
- Appears to be underage
- Shares your private information
- Uses fake photos
- Engages in hate speech
- Violates Community Guidelines
- Makes you feel unsafe
- Scams or defrauds you

### 6.2 How to Report

**In the App:**
1. Go to the user's profile
2. Tap the menu icon (•••)
3. Select "Report"
4. Choose the reason
5. Provide details
6. Submit

**Via Email:**
- Safety issues: safety@flamoral.com
- Abuse: abuse@flamoral.com
- Scams: scams@flamoral.com

**What to include:**
- User's profile (username, link)
- Screenshots of conversations
- Description of the issue
- Any relevant details
- Your contact information (if comfortable)

### 6.3 Blocking Users

**When to Block:**
- They make you uncomfortable
- They're harassing you
- You don't want to interact with them
- After reporting them

**How to Block:**
1. Go to their profile
2. Tap the menu icon (•••)
3. Select "Block"
4. Confirm

**What happens when you block:**
- They can't see your profile
- They can't message you
- They won't know they're blocked
- You won't see them in your matches

### 6.4 If You're Being Harassed

**Steps to take:**
1. **Block the user** immediately
2. **Report** to Flamoral
3. **Save evidence** (screenshots, messages)
4. **Tell someone** you trust
5. **Contact police** if you feel threatened
6. **Document everything**

**If harassment continues off-platform:**
- Block on all platforms
- Report to each platform
- Consider legal action (restraining order)
- File police report if threats made
- Contact FBI if it crosses state lines

## 8. Additional Safety Tips

### 8.1 LGBTQ+ Safety

**Additional considerations:**
- Come out on your own timeline
- Be aware of local laws and attitudes
- Meet in LGBTQ+-friendly spaces when possible
- Tell friends about your date
- Research safe venues
- Trust your community

**Resources:**
- **The Trevor Project:** 1-866-488-7386
- **LGBT National Hotline:** 1-888-843-4564
- **Trans Lifeline:** 1-877-565-8860

### 8.2 Safety for People with Disabilities

- Choose accessible meeting locations
- Communicate any accessibility needs
- Bring a mobility aid or service animal as needed
- Don't feel pressured to disclose more than you're comfortable with
- Report ableist behavior

### 8.3 Traveling or Dating While Traveling

**Extra precautions:**
- Share detailed itinerary with someone at home
- Research local safety and laws
- Meet only in well-populated tourist areas
- Know location of nearest embassy/consulate
- Keep copies of important documents
- Have local emergency numbers saved
- Be extra cautious in unfamiliar areas

### 8.4 Safety for Single Parents

**Protecting your children:**
- Don't share information about your children initially
- Don't include children in early profile photos
- Don't introduce dates to children quickly (wait several months)
- Never leave children alone with a date
- Trust your parental instincts
- Background check if relationship becomes serious

## 9. Trust Your Instincts

**The Most Important Safety Tip:**

Your intuition is powerful. If something feels wrong, it probably is.

**Trust yourself when:**
- You feel uncomfortable
- Someone seems too good to be true
- Your gut says to be cautious
- Red flags appear
- You're being pressured
- Something doesn't add up

**It's always okay to:**
- Say no
- Change your mind
- Leave a situation
- Block someone
- Report someone
- Choose your safety over politeness

**You are never obligated to:**
- Continue a conversation
- Meet someone in person
- Stay on a date
- Share personal information
- Justify your feelings
- Explain why you're uncomfortable
